My new fictional paper, “Responsible Innovation in Private Laboratories”, considers a question I initially hoped could be settled by locking the door: what does responsible independence actually require?

The answer involves more than permission to begin. Monitoring, stopping criteria and a clear plan for unexpected outcomes belong in experimental design, rather than in a hurried conversation afterwards.

As an early career researcher, I am learning that a persuasive research programme must explain both what it hopes to achieve and how it will respond if events depart from the plan.

The clipboard has therefore been admitted to the laboratory. It is not yet permitted to rearrange the apparatus, but relations are improving.
